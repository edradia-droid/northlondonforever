David Jack

David Bone Nightingale Jack (1898–1958) was an English footballer who played as an inside forward for Arsenal F.C. and Bolton Wanderers F.C.. He became one of the most accomplished forwards of the interwar period and the first player in world football to command a transfer fee exceeding £10,000.

Early career and Bolton success

Jack began his senior career at Plymouth Argyle, managed by his father Bob Jack, before joining Bolton Wanderers in 1920. At Bolton, he scored over 140 league goals and became the first player to score at Wembley Stadium, netting in the 1923 FA Cup Final. He also scored the winner in the 1926 final and earned nine caps for England.

Arsenal years

In 1928, Arsenal manager Herbert Chapman signed Jack for a record £10,890—football’s first five-figure transfer. Jack quickly became integral to Arsenal’s rise, helping secure their first major trophies. He scored 124 goals in 208 appearances, winning the FA Cup in 1930 and three First Division titles (1930–31, 1932–33, 1933–34).

Playing style and legacy

Jack was known for his precise shooting, positional intelligence, and leadership. He was one of only three players to score 100 or more top-flight goals for two clubs, alongside Jimmy Greaves and Alan Shearer.

Later life and management

After retiring in 1934, Jack managed Southend United, Middlesbrough, and Ireland’s Shelbourne. Remembered as one of Arsenal’s early icons and an FA Cup pioneer, he bridged the sport’s transformation into the professional modern era.
