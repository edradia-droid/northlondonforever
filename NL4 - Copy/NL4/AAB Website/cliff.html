Cliff Bastin

Clifford Sydney Bastin (14 March 1912 – 4 December 1991) was an English footballer best known as a prolific left winger for Arsenal F.C. in the 1930s. Renowned for his pace, power, and finishing, he was Arsenal’s record goal-scorer for six decades and a key figure in their first golden era.

Early career and Arsenal breakthrough

Bastin debuted for Exeter City F.C. at 16 and was quickly signed by Arsenal F.C. manager Herbert Chapman in 1929 for £2,000. He made his mark immediately, scoring twice in his first home match and helping Arsenal to their maiden FA Cup victory in 1930. His youth earned him the nickname “Boy Bastin.”

Peak years and honors

Partnering with playmaker Alex James, Bastin became central to Arsenal’s dominance under Chapman and George Allison. Between 1930 and 1938 he won five Football League First Division titles and two FA Cups, scoring 33 goals in 1932–33 alone. By age 19 he had league, cup, and international honors.

England career and wartime interruption

For England (1931–1938) Bastin earned 21 caps and 12 goals, including scoring in the “Battle of Britain” 3–2 win over Italy in 1934. Increasing deafness from a middle-ear infection limited him in later years. World War II curtailed his career; although he played wartime friendlies, official competition ceased.

Later life and legacy

After the war Bastin played briefly before retiring in 1947. He returned to Exeter to run a pub, and Exeter City F.C. later named a stand in his honor. His club scoring record of 178 goals stood until Ian Wright surpassed it in 1997. Remembered for cold-blooded composure and modern attacking instincts, Bastin remains among Arsenal’s all-time greats.
