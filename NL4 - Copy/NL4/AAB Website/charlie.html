Charlie George

Charlie George (born October 10, 1950) is a retired English footballer best known as a forward and attacking midfielder for Arsenal F.C. during the late 1960s and early 1970s. A North London native and lifelong Arsenal fan, he became an icon of English football after scoring the decisive goal in the 1971 FA Cup Final that secured Arsenal’s first League and Cup Double.

Arsenal heroics

Rising through Arsenal’s youth system, George debuted for the first team in 1969. He was instrumental in the club’s Inter-Cities Fairs Cup triumph and then achieved legendary status the following season. In the 1971 FA Cup final against Liverpool F.C., his extra-time thunderbolt sealed the match 2–1 and clinched the Double. His arms-outstretched celebration at Wembley became one of English football’s defining images.

Derby County and later clubs

After disputes with manager Bertie Mee, George joined Derby County F.C. in 1975 for £90,000. He enjoyed immediate success, scoring a memorable hat-trick against Real Madrid CF in the European Cup. He later played for Southampton F.C., Nottingham Forest F.C.—where he netted in the UEFA Super Cup win over FC Barcelona—as well as stints in Australia, the United States, and Hong Kong before retiring in 1983.

Style and legacy

George combined flair, power, and vision, known for his long-range shooting and rebellious charisma. With his long hair and anti-establishment aura, he embodied the 1970s “maverick” footballer, often compared to George Best. Though capped just once for England national football team in 1976, his impact on Arsenal’s history endures. He later returned to the club in hospitality and museum roles, symbolizing the lifelong bond between player and team.
