Jens Lehmann

Jens Gerhard Lehmann (born November 10, 1969) is a German former professional soccer goalkeeper best known for his role with Arsenal F.C.’s “Invincibles” team of 2003–04. Renowned for his sharp reflexes and fiery temperament, he also starred for the Germany national football team and leading Bundesliga clubs.

Club career and “Invincibles” legacy

Lehmann began at Schalke 04, where he won the 1997 UEFA Cup, before moving to AC Milan and then Borussia Dortmund, winning the 2002 Bundesliga. In 2003 he joined Arsenal, helping the club complete an unbeaten Premier League campaign in 2003–04. His consistency earned him the 2006 UEFA Club Goalkeeper of the Year after a record 853 minutes without conceding in the Champions League. He briefly returned to Arsenal in 2011 before retiring.

International career

Lehmann was Germany’s starting goalkeeper at the 2006 World Cup, where his penalty saves against Argentina sealed a famous quarterfinal win. He also reached the Euro 2008 final. His rivalry with Oliver Kahn was one of the defining narratives of German football in the 2000s.

Post-playing activities and controversies

Following retirement, Lehmann served as a coach and pundit, including a stint as assistant at FC Augsburg and on Arsenal’s staff. He became involved in the Laureus “Kicking Girls” initiative promoting sport among disadvantaged youth in Germany. In recent years, his public image has been clouded by personal and legal troubles, including disputes and controversial remarks that have drawn media scrutiny.

Reputation

At his peak, Lehmann was considered among Europe’s elite goalkeepers—brilliant, volatile, and fiercely competitive. His part in Arsenal’s historic unbeaten season and Germany’s 2006 World Cup run secured his place as one of the most memorable keepers of his generation.
