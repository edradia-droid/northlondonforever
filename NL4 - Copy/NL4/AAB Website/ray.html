THE RAY KENNEDY STORY

Arsenal Beginnings

Kennedy joined Arsenal in 1968 after an early setback at Port Vale. He broke into the first team in 1969 and quickly established himself as a powerful and intelligent forward. His development at Highbury would soon lead to some of the greatest achievements of Arsenal's early 1970s era.

European Glory & The Double

Kennedy scored in the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup Final to help Arsenal secure their first European trophy. In the following season he became central to the historic League and FA Cup Double, scoring 19 goals during the campaign, including the decisive header against Tottenham Hotspur at White Hart Lane that clinched the league title.

Liverpool Dominance

Kennedy transferred to Liverpool in 1974, where manager Bob Paisley transformed him into an elegant left-sided midfielder. He went on to make 393 appearances and win five First Division titles, three European Cups, the 1976 UEFA Cup and the 1981 League Cup.

Later Career

After leaving Liverpool, Kennedy played for Swansea City and Hartlepool United. His playing career was eventually brought to an end by Parkinson's disease, which he was diagnosed with in 1984.

England Career

Kennedy represented England 17 times and scored three international goals. His versatility allowed him to operate effectively in different attacking and midfield roles throughout his career.

A Lasting Legacy

Remembered for his strength, technical ability, intelligence and humility, Ray Kennedy remains a beloved figure in English football. His achievements with Arsenal and Liverpool established him as one of the outstanding players of his generation.
