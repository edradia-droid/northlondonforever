02 / HIS JOURNEY

THE PAT RICE STORY

Arsenal Beginnings

Rice joined Arsenal as an apprentice in 1964 and made his first-team debut in 1967. He steadily developed into a dependable right-back and became a first-choice member of the Arsenal defence during the early 1970s.

The 1971 Double

Rice was part of the historic Arsenal side that won the Football League First Division and FA Cup Double in 1971. His consistency, stamina and disciplined defending made him an important member of one of the greatest teams in Arsenal history.

Captain & Leader

During the late 1970s, Rice took on greater responsibility as captain. His calm personality, professionalism and understanding of the game made him a natural leader both on and off the pitch.

Watford Years

After leaving Arsenal in 1980, Rice continued his playing career with Watford. He spent several seasons with the club before retiring from professional football in 1984.

Northern Ireland

Rice earned 49 international caps for Northern Ireland between 1968 and 1979. He represented his country with the same reliability and leadership that had made him a respected figure at Arsenal, captaining Northern Ireland on several occasions.

Returning to Arsenal

After retiring as a player, Rice returned to Arsenal and became involved in youth development. His knowledge of the club and experience as a player made him an important figure in developing the next generation of Arsenal footballers.

The Wenger Era

Rice became assistant manager to Arsène Wenger in 1996. Together they helped oversee one of the most successful periods in Arsenal's history, including league title victories in 1998, 2002 and 2004.

The Invincibles

As part of Wenger's coaching staff, Rice played an important supporting role during Arsenal's unforgettable 2003–04 campaign. The team completed the Premier League season unbeaten, earning the legendary title of "The Invincibles."

A Lifetime of Service

After retiring from full-time coaching in 2012, Rice remained a deeply respected figure among Arsenal supporters. His decades of service to the club made him a symbol of loyalty, continuity and Arsenal tradition.

Recognition & Legacy

Rice was awarded an MBE for his services to sport and football. His legacy stretches across several generations, from the Double-winning side of 1971 to Arsenal's great teams under Arsène Wenger.
