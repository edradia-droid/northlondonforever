Ted Drake

Ted Drake (1912–1995) was an English footballer and manager best known as a prolific striker for Arsenal F.C. in the 1930s and later as the manager who led Chelsea F.C. to their first league title. Renowned for power, courage, and relentless scoring, he remains a legendary figure in English football history.

Arsenal career and playing achievements

Joining Arsenal from Southampton in 1934, Drake became the spearhead of Herbert Chapman’s successor George Allison’s championship side. His 1934–35 season tally of 44 goals remains among the club’s highest single-season totals. Drake’s seven-goal performance against Aston Villa F.C. in 1935 is still a joint record for the most goals by one player in a top-flight English league match. He also scored the winning goal in the 1936 FA Cup Final against Sheffield United F.C..

International recognition

Between 1934 and 1938 Drake earned five caps for England national football team, scoring six goals. His debut came in the famous “Battle of Highbury” match against Italy, where he netted the decisive goal.

Managerial career

After wartime service in the Royal Air Force, Drake turned to management, guiding Reading F.C. to near promotion before transforming Chelsea. In 1954–55 he led the club to their first Football League First Division title—becoming the first person to win England’s top league both as a player and manager. He modernized Chelsea’s identity, introducing the “Blues” nickname and removing the “Pensioners” badge emblem.

Legacy

Ted Drake’s total of 139 goals in 182 appearances places him among Arsenal’s all-time leading scorers. Revered for his sportsmanship and determination, he bridged football’s pre- and post-war eras and remains a symbol of English football’s golden age.
