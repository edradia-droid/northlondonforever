ARSENAL MANAGERS
1894 — PRESENT

Each profile includes a short introduction. Select More Details for managerial tenure, matches, win percentage, trophies and three defining chapters from the manager's Arsenal story.

Historical note: early Arsenal management records are not perfectly uniform. Sam Hollis is sometimes described as a trainer rather than a professional manager, wartime football under James McEwen is not directly comparable, and caretaker spells are shown separately where appropriate.
01
TRAINER / SECRETARY-MANAGER (HISTORICAL ATTRIBUTION)

Sam Hollis

1894–1897

A pioneering figure from Woolwich Arsenal's formative years. Hollis is credited by some Arsenal histories as the club's first manager-like figure, although other records describe him primarily as the first-team trainer before the professional manager era began.

02
FIRST PROFESSIONAL MANAGER

Thomas Mitchell

1897–1898

Thomas Mitchell is widely recognised as Arsenal's first professional manager. His stay was short, but he helped move Woolwich Arsenal up the Second Division table and established a more recognisable professional management structure.

More Details →
03
MANAGER

William Elcoat

1898–1899

William Elcoat succeeded Thomas Mitchell during Arsenal's Second Division years. His single-season reign continued the club's attempt to build a competitive side from players drawn heavily from the stronger northern football regions.

More Details →
04
MANAGER

Harry Bradshaw

1899–1904

Harry Bradshaw brought greater tactical organisation and consistency to Woolwich Arsenal. Under him the club became a serious Second Division force and laid the platform for promotion to the First Division.

More Details →
05
MANAGER

Phil Kelso

1904–1908

Phil Kelso took over after Arsenal's promotion and managed the club during its first sustained spell in the top division. His sides were especially notable for deep FA Cup runs in consecutive seasons.

More Details →
06
MANAGER

George Morrell

1908–1915

George Morrell managed Arsenal through one of the most turbulent periods in club history: financial difficulty, relegation, the move from Woolwich to Highbury and the transition toward a new identity in north London.

More Details →
07
SECRETARY-MANAGER DURING WARTIME

James McEwen

1915–1919

James McEwen served Arsenal during the First World War period, when normal Football League competition was suspended. Because wartime matches were regional and unofficial for standard record purposes, his statistics cannot be compared cleanly with later managers.

More Details →
08
SECRETARY-MANAGER

Leslie Knighton

1919–1925

Leslie Knighton took charge when Arsenal returned to the First Division after the war. He kept the club in the top flight but struggled to turn it into a consistent contender, and his departure opened the door for Herbert Chapman.

More Details →
09
MANAGER

Herbert Chapman

1925–1934

Herbert Chapman transformed Arsenal into a major power and became one of football's great innovators. Tactics, training, recruitment, stadium presentation and even ideas about floodlights and numbered shirts all formed part of his modernising influence.

More Details →
10
CARETAKER MANAGER

Joe Shaw

1934 (caretaker)

Joe Shaw stepped in after Herbert Chapman's sudden death and guided Arsenal through the remainder of the 1933/34 season. His calm stewardship helped the club finish the work Chapman had begun and secure another league title.

More Details →
11
MANAGER

George Allison

1934–1947

George Allison carried Arsenal's first golden age forward after Chapman. A journalist, broadcaster and club administrator before becoming manager, he oversaw further league titles and an FA Cup while preserving the elite standards of the 1930s.

More Details →
12
MANAGER

Tom Whittaker

1947–1956

Tom Whittaker was an Arsenal player, trainer and coach before becoming manager. He rebuilt the club after the Second World War and delivered two league titles and an FA Cup during a demanding era of post-war recovery.

More Details →
13
MANAGER

Jack Crayston

1956–1958

Former Arsenal midfielder Jack Crayston succeeded Tom Whittaker. His managerial period was short, but Arsenal improved their league position and he completed more than two decades of service to the club as player, coach and manager.

More Details →
14
MANAGER

George Swindin

1958–1962

George Swindin returned to Arsenal as manager after previously making hundreds of appearances as the club's goalkeeper. His first season produced a strong third-place league finish, but the team could not turn that promise into a sustained title challenge.

More Details →
15
MANAGER

Billy Wright

1962–1966

England legend Billy Wright arrived at Arsenal with enormous stature from his playing career. His teams introduced and developed young talent but could not achieve the consistent results or trophies expected at Highbury.

More Details →
16
MANAGER

Bertie Mee

1966–1976

Bertie Mee led Arsenal out of a long trophy drought and into one of the club's defining achievements: the 1970/71 League and FA Cup Double. He also delivered Arsenal's first European trophy, the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup.

More Details →
17
MANAGER

Terry Neill

1976–1983

Terry Neill, a former Arsenal captain, returned as manager in 1976. His side reached three consecutive FA Cup finals and produced the unforgettable 1979 victory over Manchester United in the 'Five-Minute Final'.

More Details →
18
MANAGER

Don Howe

1983–1986

Don Howe was one of English football's most respected coaches. As Arsenal manager he worked through a transitional period and gave important first-team opportunities to young players who would later become central to the club's revival.

More Details →
19
CARETAKER MANAGER

Steve Burtenshaw

1986 (caretaker)

Steve Burtenshaw briefly took caretaker charge following Don Howe's departure in 1986. His role was temporary and chiefly concerned with guiding Arsenal through the transition before George Graham's appointment.

More Details →
20
MANAGER

George Graham

1986–1995

George Graham restored Arsenal as a trophy-winning force. Built on defensive discipline, organisation and a strong core of academy-connected players, his teams won two league titles, domestic cups and the 1994 European Cup Winners' Cup.

More Details →
21
CARETAKER MANAGER

Stewart Houston

1995 & 1996 (caretaker)

Stewart Houston twice acted as Arsenal caretaker manager during the mid-1990s. His first spell followed George Graham's departure and included the run to the 1995 European Cup Winners' Cup final; he returned briefly after Bruce Rioch left.

More Details →
22
MANAGER

Bruce Rioch

1995–1996

Bruce Rioch managed Arsenal for a single season. Although he won no trophy, his reign is remembered for helping the club qualify for Europe and, above all, for bringing Dennis Bergkamp to Highbury.

More Details →
23
CARETAKER MANAGER

Pat Rice

1996 (caretaker)

Arsenal legend Pat Rice briefly took caretaker charge immediately before Arsène Wenger began work. He later became Wenger's long-serving assistant and a crucial continuity figure between old Arsenal traditions and a new international era.

More Details →
24
MANAGER

Arsène Wenger

1996–2018

Arsène Wenger is Arsenal's longest-serving manager and the defining figure of the club's modern history. He transformed training, diet, recruitment and playing style, won three league titles and a record seven FA Cups, and guided Arsenal through the move from Highbury to Emirates Stadium.

More Details →
25
HEAD COACH

Unai Emery

2018–2019

Unai Emery succeeded Arsène Wenger after twenty-two years of continuity. His first season included a strong unbeaten run and a journey to the 2019 Europa League final, but inconsistent league form eventually ended his tenure in November 2019.

More Details →
26
INTERIM HEAD COACH

Freddie Ljungberg

2019 (interim)

Invincibles hero Freddie Ljungberg stepped up from Arsenal's coaching staff as interim head coach after Unai Emery left. His six-match spell was a holding period while the club completed the appointment of Mikel Arteta.

More Details →
27
MANAGER

Mikel Arteta

2019–present

Mikel Arteta returned to Arsenal as head coach in December 2019 after serving as Pep Guardiola's assistant at Manchester City. A former Arsenal captain, he reshaped the squad and playing identity, winning the FA Cup in his first season and later leading the club back to the Premier League summit.

More Details →
