Clifford Sydney Bastin was an English footballer best known as a prolific left winger for Arsenal in the 1930s. Renowned for his pace, power, and finishing, he was Arsenal’s record goal-scorer for six decades and a key figure in their first golden era.

THE CLIFF STORY

Early Career & Arsenal Breakthrough

Bastin debuted for Exeter City F.C. at 16 and was quickly signed by Arsenal manager Herbert Chapman in 1929 for £2,000. He made his mark immediately, scoring twice in his first home match and helping Arsenal to their maiden FA Cup victory in 1930. His youth earned him the nickname “Boy Bastin.”

Peak Years & Honors

Partnering with playmaker Alex James, Bastin became central to Arsenal’s dominance under Chapman and George Allison. Between 1930 and 1938 he won five Football League First Division titles and two FA Cups, scoring 33 goals in 1932–33 alone.

England, War & Legacy

For England, Bastin earned 21 caps and 12 goals, including scoring in the “Battle of Britain” win over Italy in 1934. Increasing deafness and World War II curtailed his career. After the war he played briefly before retiring in 1947. His 178 Arsenal goals stood until Ian Wright surpassed the record in 1997.
