Lee Dixon

Lee Dixon is a former English professional footballer best known as Arsenal’s long-serving right-back from 1988 to 2002. A cornerstone of the club’s famed defensive line, he helped define Arsenal’s success under managers George Graham and Arsène Wenger, later becoming a respected television pundit.

Arsenal career and achievements

Dixon joined Arsenal in January 1988 and played 619 matches over nearly 15 years, ranking among the club’s top appearance-makers. He won four league titles—including the historic 1988–89 championship clinched at Anfield—three FA Cups, a League Cup, and the 1994 European Cup Winners’ Cup. Renowned for his positional intelligence and composure, he was part of the “famous back four” alongside Tony Adams, Steve Bould, and Nigel Winterburn.

England representation

Between 1990 and 1999, Dixon earned 22 caps for England, contributing solid defensive performances but facing stiff competition for the right-back role. His international career included appearances in both friendlies and qualifying campaigns.

Post-retirement and media work

After retiring in 2002, Dixon transitioned into broadcasting. He worked with the BBC on Match of the Day, Final Score, and Football Focus, later joining ITV Sport and becoming a Premier League analyst for NBC Sports in the United States from 2013. Known for insightful and balanced commentary, he remains a respected voice in football media.

Legacy

Dixon is remembered as one of Arsenal’s most dependable defenders and a model of consistency. His professionalism, tactical acumen, and longevity helped set standards for the modern full-back role, making him a revered figure among supporters and peers alike.
