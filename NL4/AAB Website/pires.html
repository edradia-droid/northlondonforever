Robert Pirès

Robert Pirès is a retired French footballer renowned for his elegant play as a left midfielder and attacking winger. A key figure in Arsenal F.C.’s early-2000s dominance and a member of France’s 1998 World Cup–winning squad, he is celebrated for technical skill, vision, and consistency.

Career and playing style

Pirès began professionally with FC Metz in 1992, winning the 1996 League Cup before joining Olympique de Marseille. In 2000 he transferred to Arsenal, where under Arsène Wenger he became central to the “French Connection” era. His combination play, movement, and precise finishing helped define the club’s fluid attacking style.

Arsenal and the “Invincibles”

Between 2000 and 2006, Pirès played 284 matches and scored 84 goals for Arsenal. He twice won the Premier League (2001–02, 2003–04) and three FA Cups (2002, 2003, 2005). The 2003–04 season—when Arsenal went unbeaten—cemented his legacy as part of the “Invincibles.” He was named PFA Team of the Year three times and earned the 2002 Footballer of the Year award in England

International success

For France, Pirès won the 1998 FIFA World Cup and the 2000 UEFA European Championship alongside stars such as Zinedine Zidane and Thierry Henry. He added two FIFA Confederations Cup titles and was included in Pelé’s FIFA 100 list of greatest living players in 2004

Later years and legacy

After Arsenal, Pirès joined Villarreal CF (2006–2010) and Aston Villa F.C. before retiring in 2015 following a final stint with FC Goa. Known for sportsmanship and technical grace, he remains an ambassador for Arsenal and French football, frequently participating in charity matches and coaching initiatives
